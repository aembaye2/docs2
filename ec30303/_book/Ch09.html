2  Chapter 13: The Costs of Production

Intermediate Micro Study Notes and Practice

2.1 1. Economic vs. Accounting Costs

  • Explicit costs are payments made to outsiders: wages, rent, materials, interest on borrowed funds.
  • Implicit costs are the value of resources the owner already controls and does not pay for directly, such as the owner’s forgone salary, or interest the owner could have earned on savings used in the business.

Economic cost, accounting profit, and economic profit are related as follows:

\[ \text{Economic cost} = \text{Explicit costs} + \text{Implicit costs} \]

\[ \pi_{\text{accounting}} = TR - \text{Explicit costs} \]

\[ \pi_{\text{economic}} = TR - (\text{Explicit costs} + \text{Implicit costs}) = \pi_{\text{accounting}} - \text{Implicit costs} \]

  • An economic profit of zero is not a failure. It means the owner earns exactly what the resources could earn in their next-best use (a normal return).
  • Sunk costs are costs already committed that cannot be recovered. They should not affect forward-looking decisions.

2.2 2. Production Function and Short-Run Costs

  • A production function links inputs to output, for example \(Q = f(L, K)\).
  • In the short run, at least one input is fixed (for example, the factory). In the long run, every input can be changed.
  • Marginal product of labor: \(MP_L = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta L}\)
  • Diminishing marginal product: after some point, each added worker adds less output than the one before, given fixed capital.

Cost definitions, with \(FC\) = fixed cost and \(VC\) = variable cost:

\[ TC = FC + VC \]

\[ AFC = \frac{FC}{Q}, \qquad AVC = \frac{VC}{Q}, \qquad ATC = \frac{TC}{Q} = AFC + AVC \]

\[ MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q} = \frac{\Delta VC}{\Delta Q} \]

  • Link between MC and MP: if labor is the only variable input and the wage is \(w\), then

    \[ MC = \frac{w}{MP_L} \]

    When \(MP_L\) rises, \(MC\) falls. When \(MP_L\) falls, \(MC\) rises. This is why the marginal cost curve rises in the short run: it reflects diminishing marginal product, with input prices held constant.

  • \(AFC\) always falls as output rises, because fixed cost is spread over more units.

  • \(AVC\), \(ATC\), and \(MC\) are typically U-shaped.

2.3 3. Key Relationships Between the Curves

  • If \(MC < ATC\), ATC is falling. If \(MC > ATC\), ATC is rising.
  • Therefore, MC crosses ATC at the minimum of ATC.
  • Similarly, MC crosses AVC at the minimum of AVC.
  • The minimum of ATC occurs at a higher output than the minimum of AVC, because ATC also includes \(AFC\), which is still falling at that point.
  • A change in fixed cost shifts \(AFC\) and \(ATC\), but does not change \(MC\) or \(AVC\).

2.4 4. Long-Run Costs

  • In the long run, all inputs are variable, so the firm can choose its best plant size.
  • The long-run average total cost (LRATC) curve is the envelope of the short-run ATC curves: it touches each SRATC curve at one point.
  • Economies of scale: LRATC falls as output rises (specialization, division of labor, cheaper bulk inputs).
  • Constant returns to scale: LRATC is flat.
  • Diseconomies of scale: LRATC rises as output rises (coordination problems, management layers, bureaucracy).
  • Minimum efficient scale (MES): the smallest output at which LRATC reaches its minimum.

2.5 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating accounting profit as economic profit. Always subtract the implicit cost of the owner’s time and money.
  • Including sunk costs in a decision about the future.
  • Saying MC rises “because costs rise.” MC rises because marginal product falls (with input prices held constant).
  • Reading the ATC minimum from the MC curve without checking that MC crosses ATC there.
  • Thinking that a fixed-cost increase raises MC. It does not.

2.6 Practice Questions

2.6.1 Part A: Multiple Choice

Question 1. A bakery’s weekly short-run data are shown below.

Output (loaves) 0 10 20 30 40 50
Total cost ($) 600 900 1,100 1,350 1,650 2,150

Which statement is correct?

  • A. ATC is lowest at 20 loaves, because MC is lowest over the interval from 10 to 20.
  • B. ATC is lowest at 40 loaves (about $41.25 per loaf), and ATC rises to $43 at 50 loaves.
  • C. ATC is lowest at 30 loaves, because MC equals ATC at that output.
  • D. Fixed cost is $1,100 per week.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

ATC at each output: 10 loaves → 90, 20 → 55, 30 → 45, 40 → 41.25, 50 → 43. ATC is lowest at 40 loaves. MC over each interval is 30, 20, 25, 30, 50. MC is lowest between 10 and 20, but that does not determine the ATC minimum. Fixed cost is $600 (the TC at zero output), so D is wrong.

Question 2. A small business owner’s accounting profit for the year is $80,000. She could have earned $50,000 working for an employer, and the $400,000 she has invested in the business could have earned $30,000 in interest elsewhere. Which statement is correct?

  • A. Her economic profit is $80,000, because the business is profitable by accounting standards.
  • B. Her economic profit is zero, because implicit costs total $80,000.
  • C. Her economic profit is −$30,000, because the implicit costs total $110,000.
  • D. Her economic profit is $30,000, because only the interest on capital is an implicit cost.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

Implicit costs = forgone salary ($50,000) + forgone interest ($30,000) = $80,000. Economic profit = accounting profit − implicit costs = $80,000 − $80,000 = 0. The owner earns a normal return: the business is just as good as her next-best use of her time and money.

Question 3. A firm hires workers at a fixed wage. The marginal product of labor rises from the first worker to the third worker and then falls for each additional worker. Which statement is correct?

  • A. Marginal cost falls as long as marginal product rises, then rises once marginal product begins to fall.
  • B. Marginal cost rises from the first worker onward, because the wage is constant.
  • C. Marginal cost is constant, because the wage is constant.
  • D. Marginal cost falls indefinitely, because more workers always lower the cost per unit.
Answer Key

Answer: A.

Because \(MC = w / MP_L\) with a fixed wage, MC moves opposite to MP. MC falls while MP rises and rises once MP falls. The wage does not change, so it cannot explain the rise in MC.

Question 4. A firm’s long-run average total cost falls from an output of 100 units to 300 units, stays flat from 300 to 500 units, and rises beyond 500 units. Which statement is correct?

  • A. Diseconomies of scale begin at 300 units.
  • B. The minimum efficient scale is 300 units.
  • C. There are constant returns to scale below 300 units.
  • D. The minimum efficient scale is 500 units, where the curve starts to rise.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

Economies of scale operate from 100 to 300 units. Between 300 and 500 units LRATC is flat (constant returns to scale). Diseconomies begin above 500 units. The minimum efficient scale is the smallest output that reaches the minimum LRATC, which is 300 units.

Question 5. A firm in a competitive market has a minimum AVC of $14 (at 8 units) and a minimum ATC of $20 (at 12 units). The market price is $12. In the short run, which is correct?

  • A. The firm should keep producing at 12 units, because price is below minimum ATC but the firm can still cover fixed costs.
  • B. The firm should shut down, because price is below minimum AVC, so it cannot cover variable costs.
  • C. The firm should produce 8 units, because that is where AVC is at its minimum.
  • D. The firm should expand output to reach the lowest ATC at 12 units.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

In the short run, a firm should keep operating only if price covers AVC at its chosen output. With price of $12 below the lowest AVC of $14, no output level covers variable costs, so the firm shuts down and loses only its fixed cost. The ATC comparison is not the short-run shutdown test; A and D reflect that mistake.

Question 6. At an output of 150 units, a firm’s ATC is $24 and ATC is falling as output rises. Which must be true at 150 units?

  • A. MC is greater than $24.
  • B. MC is less than $24.
  • C. MC equals $24.
  • D. AFC is rising.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

ATC falls only when MC is below ATC (the new unit pulls the average down). So MC < $24 at 150 units. AFC always falls as output rises, so D is wrong.

Question 7. A firm spent $2 million last year on specialized equipment that has no resale value. It is now deciding whether to keep operating. Which is correct?

  • A. The $2 million should be included in the decision, because it is a cost the firm has already incurred.
  • B. The $2 million should be excluded, because it is a sunk cost; only future revenues and avoidable costs should be compared.
  • C. The $2 million should be divided over future output to find a higher ATC that justifies shutting down.
  • D. The $2 million should be counted as an implicit cost that reduces the firm’s accounting profit.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

Sunk costs cannot be recovered whether the firm continues or stops, so they should not affect the forward-looking decision. Option D is wrong because the cost is explicit (it was paid), and it is already a past cost, not a forgone opportunity.

Question 8. A firm’s monthly rent rises by $200, with no change in output or variable inputs. Which is correct?

  • A. MC rises by $200 / Q, and AVC rises.
  • B. AFC and ATC both rise, while MC and AVC are unchanged.
  • C. MC and ATC both rise, while AFC falls.
  • D. Only AVC rises, because rent is a variable cost.
Answer Key

Answer: B.

Rent is a fixed cost. A higher FC raises AFC (by $200 divided by Q) and therefore ATC, but it does not change VC, AVC, or MC.

2.6.2 Part B: Calculation Questions

Question 9. A firm has fixed cost of $50. Its total cost at outputs Q = 1 to 5 is given in the R table below. The table is generated from the total cost values, so you can check every entry yourself.

  1. Using the table, find the output at which ATC is at its minimum, and its value.
  2. Verify that \(ATC = AFC + AVC\) at \(Q = 3\).
Answer Key

(a) ATC is lowest at \(Q = 4\), where \(ATC = \$40.00\).

(b) At \(Q = 3\): \(AFC + AVC = 16.67 + 25.00 = 41.67 = ATC = 125 / 3 = 41.67\). ✓

Question 10. A firm has fixed cost of $500 and hires labor at $100 per day. The R table below shows output for 0 to 5 workers and the resulting marginal product and marginal cost.

  1. Using the table, describe how MC changes as MP changes.
  2. At 5 workers, compute AVC and ATC.
Answer Key

(a) MC = \(w / MP_L\). MC falls from worker 1 to worker 2 while MP rises, then rises from worker 3 onward once MP falls.

(b) At 5 workers: \(VC = 5 \times 100 = \$500\) and \(TC = 500 + 500 = \$1{,}000\). \(AVC = 500 / 42 \approx \$11.90\). \(ATC = 1{,}000 / 42 \approx \$23.81\).

Question 11. A firm has the cost function

\[ TC = 200 + 10Q + 0.5Q^2 \]

The R table below evaluates ATC and MC for outputs from 10 to 30 units.

  1. Find expressions for \(FC\), \(VC\), \(AVC\), \(ATC\), and \(MC\).
  2. Compute \(TC\), \(AVC\), \(ATC\), and \(MC\) at \(Q = 20\).
  3. Find the output that minimizes \(ATC\), and the minimum \(ATC\) value.
Answer Key

(a)

\[ FC = 200, \qquad VC = 10Q + 0.5Q^2 \]

\[ AVC = 10 + 0.5Q, \qquad ATC = \frac{200}{Q} + 10 + 0.5Q, \qquad MC = \frac{dTC}{dQ} = 10 + Q \]

(b) At \(Q = 20\):

  • \(TC = 200 + 200 + 200 = \$600\)
  • \(AVC = 10 + 10 = \$20\)
  • \(ATC = 600 / 20 = \$30\)
  • \(MC = 10 + 20 = \$30\)

(c) Set the derivative of ATC to zero:

\[ -\frac{200}{Q^2} + 0.5 = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad Q^2 = 400 \quad\Rightarrow\quad Q = 20 \]

Minimum ATC \(= \frac{200}{20} + 10 + 10 = \$30\). This matches the point where \(MC = ATC\). In the table, ATC at \(Q = 20\) is the lowest value.

Question 12. A firm’s long-run average total cost is $50 at 100 units, $40 at 200 units, $40 at 400 units, and $52 at 800 units.

  1. Over what output range does the firm experience economies of scale, constant returns, and diseconomies of scale?
  2. What is the minimum efficient scale?
Answer Key

(a) Economies of scale: 100 to 200 units (LRATC falls from $50 to $40). Constant returns: 200 to 400 units (LRATC flat at $40). Diseconomies of scale: beyond 400 units (LRATC rises to $52 at 800 units).

(b) The minimum efficient scale is 200 units, the smallest output at which LRATC reaches its minimum of $40.

Question 13. A firm has the short-run total cost

\[ TC = 100 + 4Q + Q^2, \qquad MC = 4 + 2Q \]

  1. If the market price is $20, how many units should the firm produce, and what is its profit?
  2. If the market price falls to $10, should the firm produce or shut down? Explain using the loss from each choice.
Answer Key

(a) Set \(P = MC\): \(20 = 4 + 2Q\), so \(Q = 8\).

  • \(TR = 20 \times 8 = \$160\)
  • \(TC = 100 + 32 + 64 = \$196\)
  • Profit \(= 160 - 196 = \mathbf{-\$36}\)

The firm operates because price ($20) covers AVC at \(Q = 8\) (AVC \(= 4 + 8 = \$12\)). Shutting down would lose the full $100 of fixed cost, so producing (loss of $36) is better.

(b) Set \(P = MC\): \(10 = 4 + 2Q\), so \(Q = 3\).

  • \(TR = 10 \times 3 = \$30\)
  • \(TC = 100 + 12 + 9 = \$121\)
  • Profit \(= 30 - 121 = \mathbf{-\$91}\)

Price ($10) is above AVC at \(Q = 3\) (AVC \(= 4 + 3 = \$7\)), so the firm still covers its variable cost and produces. Its loss of $91 is less than the $100 loss from shutting down, so the firm should keep producing in the short run.
